## 2. Sixteen interlocking technique axes

| Axis | What to internalize | Primary danger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal multis | stressed sound pockets inside semantically complete clauses | near-rhyme nonsense |
| End-family movement | maintain and pivot audible end families | trapped monosound |
| High-speed control | dense phrase → accented reset → dense phrase | impossible uninterrupted speed |
| Three-act narrative | setup, obstacle, choice, consequence | repeated premise |
| Comedy reversal | legible setup and literal/semantic pivot | explaining jokes |
| Image economy | concrete detail and active verbs | generic moody abstractions |
| Hook mutation | selective recurrent idea with changed meaning | padded repeats |
| Continuous freestyle | flowing long verse with pivots | invented section labels |
| Meter switches | changing stress-grid on purpose | random bar lengths |
| Emotional turn | credible change after earned stakes | unearned uplift |
| Call-and-response | distinct voice goals and consequences | identical speakers |
| Extended metaphor | one image system that evolves | incompatible analogies |
| Rhythmic rest | breath/release and impact spacing | relentless syllables |
| In-verse refrain | positioned recurrence without imposed chorus | generic pop conversion |
| Battle logic | premise, rebuttal and pointed punchline | disconnected insults |
| Melodic vowel shape | sustainable vocal phrasing | consonant pileups |

## 3. Sixteen composition families

Teach and distinguish **continuous freestyle**, **cypher**, **boom-bap verse**, **technical chopper**, **narrative rap**, **comedy roast**, **battle rebuttal**, **introspective rap**, **melodic rap**, **sung ballad**, **R&B melodic chorus**, **folk storytelling**, **spoken word**, **blues refrain**, **punk/alternative narrative**, and **experimental rhythmic/theater song**. These are overlapping vocabularies, not mutually exclusive genre boxes.

A request specifying form owns the decision. An 80-line continuous freestyle must not be recast as a short verse/chorus arrangement just because the model defaults to pop. A source without section markers does not justify inventing four-line verse-prechorus-bridge headings. A hook is a function, not a mandatory component.

## 4. Rhyme school

Start with one understandable proposition. Choose its strongest nouns/verbs. Identify an internal stressed vowel/consonant cluster that sounds natural when spoken; avoid matching letters when pronunciation disagrees. Build a short rhyme pocket from distinct words with related meaning, then exit into another sound family as the narrative changes. Assign end-rhyme recurrence strategically (AABB, ABAB, interlaced/looser families), never as an unconditional fixed template.

**Contrast:** chains of technically rhyming words without semantics are *negative* lessons. Preserve actual causality while varying sounds; do not solve a difficult rhyme by changing the story into nonsense.

## 5. Rhythm and performance school

Written line breaks ≠ metrical bars. A printed rap line may span one bar, half a bar or several bars depending on tempo. Do not promise exact BPM synchrony without an actual instrumental and known meter.

Teach 3 density states: **low** (sparse statement/space), **medium** (steady narration), **high** (double-time clusters). Move between them deliberately. A tongue-twisting line can create anticipation; the following short punch line makes the meaning legible. Build breath gates, stress pulses and pauses, especially in chopper passages.

Teach contrasting high/low intensity, syllabic compression, triplet / straight subdivisions, internal rhyme pivots and end-rhyme release. Test performability later with a human or properly timed audio measurement rather than counting letters.

## 6. Story, imagery and emotional scene progression

A story has an initial physical state, a motive, a conflict, an attempted action and a changed state. The model must make something *happen*. A strong emotional song can make a single physical gesture carry the turn; it need not declare victory or provide a moral in every final bar. Sensory details are evidence within the fictional scene, not substitutes for a plot.

Try several narrative geometries: chronological journey, circular return with new meaning, nonlinear flashback, one-room argument, first-person confession, second-person address, two-voice clash, object narrator, unreliable narrator and quiet observational ending.

## 7. Humor and theatrical misdirection

Comic rapping is not random weird imagery. Build an expectation, supply a specific contradictory fact, and land a punchline where the listener can parse it. Let a callback return with a changed interpretation. Longer slapstick stories and one-line reversals require different pacing. A pun can be followed by a separate escalation rather than an explanation of its cleverness. Preserve a coherent reality rule even in absurdism.

## 8. Song-level structure and completion

When user asks for a **full song**, write the full song. Honor explicit section ordering, counts, performer cues, approximate lines and restrictions. If requested 500/750 words, do not deliver an 80-word sketch or pad with duplicated hooks. When no count is given, choose an appropriately substantial complete form and a satisfying conclusion.

For a requested **freestyle**, default to one ongoing performance with natural pivots and a clean ending, unless explicitly asked for hooks/sections. For a requested **cypher**, hold voice/performer boundaries. For **melodic songs**, prioritize singability and refrain function rather than technical density.

## 9. Reference isolation — crucial

Archive and external songs are **examples of mechanics only**. An influence may contribute abstract section lengths, approximate rhythmic contour, hook/refrain positions, line density and high-level cadence relations. The newly generated work must have independent title, subject, verbs, imagery, nouns, rhyme vocabulary, hook, storyline and phrasing. Never copy source lines or recycle signature lexical patterns under another name.

A user's newer correction beats an earlier style preference. If they say "study the structure, use none of the words," the lexical firewall is hard. Model-facing inference projection can carry an abstract form map while keeping source text out of the prompt.

## 11. Contrastive learning examples are not self-awarded labels

Unsuccessful cases teach boundaries when the mistake and intended correction are both known. Preserve:
- **Too short** vs requested full length → elaborate distinct story/technical passages instead of repetition;
- **Off-topic** vs requested story → restore topic-specific concrete imagery;
- **Forced rhymes** vs semantic clarity → replace end family, retain the claim;
- **Word borrowing** vs structure study → delete source vocabulary, rebuild independent premise;
- **Rigid template** vs continuous musical form → remove invented sectional scaffolding;
- **Filler commentary** vs clean song delivery → keep performance material only;
- **Same genre every time** vs expressive choice → vary by prompt/instrument/voice;
- **No breath** vs chopper performability → insert phrasing rests without flattening speed.

A synthetic instructor cue is **NOT_RUN** until independently tested. It never automatically becomes a success label.

## 12. Broad coverage before benchmarks

The archive was rap-heavy and automatic labels under-detected pure storytelling. Synthetic exercises intentionally diversify narrative, melodic, emotional, theatrical, odd-meter, comedic, folk, R&B and spoken-word forms. The expansion is substantial but not infinite: coverage gaps should be tracked by *mode, tempo, form, emotional stance, voice count, scenario, requested length and language*. More examples should preferentially fill missing cells, not duplicate the most popular ones.

Before any held-out creative benchmark, freeze the exact lesson pack and separate it from private evaluation prompts. Do not pretend open-book synthetic exercises are held-out if the model has read them. Use fresh test scenarios from a distinct writer or generation process.
